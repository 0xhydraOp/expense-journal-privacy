Privacy policy

Effective 9 October 2026 · Android package com.expensejrnl.service

Who this policy covers

0xHydra publishes Expence Journal, an Android app that turns supported transaction SMS into spending and incoming-money records. This policy describes the current release, in which transaction analysis and storage are local and cloud backup is disabled. We will update this policy before enabling different data practices in a public release.

SMS access and local processing

After you accept the in-app explanation and grant Android permission, the app reads retained SMS history and checks new messages for financial transactions. Processing can run while the app is closed or not actively used, subject to Android scheduling and message delivery.

Android SMS permission provides broad access, rather than access only to transaction messages. The app processes message text, sender information and receipt metadata on your device to identify financial candidates. Unrelated messages and OTPs are discarded from the processing flow; full message bodies are not saved in the app database or sent to us.

The app does not send SMS, make payments, connect to bank accounts, or read contacts, call logs or location.

What the app keeps

Local records can include the amount and currency, merchant, category, debit/credit or other transaction type, reported status, payment channel, account suffix, transaction references, dates, sender/source identifiers, and reasons why a field or classification is uncertain. Processing status and permission-consent preferences are also stored locally.

Historical manual records from earlier development versions may remain on the phone, including their notes. They stay separate from SMS-only spending totals. No manual-entry product workflow is provided in the current app.

SMS-derived totals may be incomplete or inaccurate and are not a bank balance. Unknown dates and classifications are identified rather than presented as verified bank information.

Data leaving your phone

The current public-release configuration does not transmit your SMS bodies or extracted financial records to our servers. Cloud backup and recovery network actions are disabled in this configuration. There are no app accounts, advertising SDKs, analytics SDKs or developer-operated automatic crash-report uploads in this release.

Private development builds may provide opt-in encrypted backup to a Cloudflare staging service. That service is not enabled in the current release. Production backup, retention and deletion practices will be described before a release enables them.

Retention, deletion and recovery

There is no automatic expiry period for local records. They remain in private app storage until that storage is cleared or the app is uninstalled. Revoking SMS permission stops future app access; it does not remove existing records.

You can revoke permission in Android Settings. To erase local app records, use Android Settings → Apps → Expence Journal → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the app. These actions do not delete the original messages from your phone's SMS app. Clearing app storage or uninstalling currently loses the app's local records; the release has no working cloud restore.

The app excludes its database, preferences and files from Android automatic cloud backup and device-transfer rules. This does not guarantee protection against every manufacturer behavior, device compromise or privileged access path. We do not claim that the local financial database is encrypted.

Privacy inquiries

Publisher: 0xHydra. You can submit general privacy questions through our GitHub Issues contact page. GitHub may require its own account to post; Expence Journal does not require one.

Issues are public. Never post SMS bodies, financial records, account details, recovery keys or passwords. Ask a general question first if your concern involves private information. We cannot access or remotely delete the local records on your phone.

This website and policy updates

This policy website has no embedded analytics, tracking scripts or external fonts. GitHub hosts the website and contact page and may process normal connection information, such as your IP address and browser information, under GitHub's privacy statement. Information you choose to post in a GitHub issue is public and available to us and other visitors.

Material changes to app data practices will be reflected here and, where applicable, in the app's disclosure and consent flow. The effective date above identifies this policy version.
